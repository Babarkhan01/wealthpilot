Mitigation: Set calendar reminders; reserve cash in a sinking fund to pay the incremental IRMAA in year T+2; document the trade-off in the financial plan.

🔎 DETAILED ANALYSIS

The couple's $235,000 MAGI already exceeds the 2026 IRMAA base tier threshold of $218,000 MFJ, placing them in IRMAA Tier 1. Each spouse pays a Part B premium of $284.10/month ($3,409/year) plus a Part D surcharge of $14.50/month. Any additional income — including Roth conversions — pushes them deeper into or across IRMAA tiers, with the next cliff at $274,000 (Tier 2).

Conversion capacity before the next IRMAA tier: roughly $39,000 ($274,000 − $235,000). Because IRMAA uses a two-year lookback, a 2026 conversion affects 2028 Medicare premiums. This creates a planning window: if either spouse is not yet 63 in 2026, conversions done before age 63 will not trigger IRMAA (since Medicare enrollment begins at 65). If both are already enrolled, every dollar of conversion income must be weighed against the IRMAA cost two years forward.

Federal rate context: the OBBBA (2025) made current federal brackets permanent — the "TCJA sunset" many older plans budgeted for is no longer a live risk. This report treats the current bracket structure as durable rather than temporary.

Ohio state and local tax: Ohio moved to a flat 2.75% state income tax on income above $26,050 starting January 1, 2026 (HB96). Converted amounts are taxed at this rate on top of the federal 24% shown above. Municipal tax adds further cost depending on city — Columbus and Cleveland levy 2.5%, for example — and is withheld separately from state tax.

Multi-year strategy: spread conversions across multiple years to stay within a target IRMAA tier, especially in years before RMDs begin (age 73 or 75 depending on birth year). Use low-income years — such as early retirement before Social Security or pension starts — to convert larger amounts. Once age 70½, QCDs (up to $111,000 per spouse in 2026) can satisfy RMDs without adding to MAGI, preserving conversion room.

Key trade-off: pay federal + state tax plus a temporary IRMAA surcharge now to eliminate future RMDs taxed at potentially higher rates, and avoid lifetime IRMAA exposure on those RMDs. The break-even depends on the couple's longevity, future tax rates, and estate goals.
